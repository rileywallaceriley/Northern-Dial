Headstones

Kingston, Ontario, Canada

Headstones emerged from Kingston, Ontario, with frontman Hugh Dillon's confrontational delivery giving the band a harder, punk-informed edge than much of Canada's early-1990s alternative-rock boom. After signing with MCA, the group broke nationally with the 1993 debut Picture of Health, whose songs included "Cemetery" and "When Something Stands for Nothing." The album eventually went platinum in Canada, establishing Headstones as both a forceful live act and a dependable presence on Canadian rock radio.

The 1995 follow-up Teeth and Tissue went gold and earned a JUNO nomination for Best Rock Album, while the next phase of the catalogue produced further radio staples and reinforced the band's mix of hard-rock weight, punk abrasion and dark humour. Dillon's work in films such as Dance Me Outside and Hard Core Logo also gave the group's music a broader cultural context, linking Headstones to a distinctly Canadian strain of rough-edged independent film and rock culture during the same period.

Headstones disbanded in 2003, then reunited in 2011 and resumed recording and touring rather than treating the comeback as a one-off nostalgia exercise. Love + Fury arrived in 2013 and earned another JUNO nomination for Rock Album of the Year, beginning a sustained second chapter that carried the band well beyond its original 1990s run. That durability is part of the Headstones story: a Kingston-born group whose abrasive identity, live intensity and national catalogue remained recognizable enough to support an active career decades after Picture of Health first broke through.
