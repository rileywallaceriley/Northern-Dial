Kinnie Starr

Calgary, Alberta, Canada

Calgary-born Kinnie Starr emerged in the mid-1990s as an unusually difficult artist to categorize, combining rap, spoken word, guitar-driven rock, pop and multilingual writing. Her 1996 debut Tidy established that hybrid approach before a major-label period with Island/Def Jam ended without a commercial album release. Rather than reshape herself around the label system, Starr returned to independent work with Tune-Up in 2000 and Sun Again in 2003. The latter helped earn her a 2004 Juno nomination for New Artist of the Year and brought a wider Canadian audience to a catalogue that had already been crossing boundaries for years.

Starr kept expanding the range of that work through albums including Anything, A Different Day, Kiss It, From Far Away and Feed the Fire. She has written, recorded, engineered and produced much of her own material, while also working with artists including Tegan and Sara and Nelly Furtado. In 2010, Starr's production work on Digging Roots' We Are... contributed to the album winning the Juno Award for Aboriginal Album of the Year. Her Mohawk ancestry has been a recurring part of her artistic perspective, but her work has resisted being confined to a single Indigenous, hip-hop or singer-songwriter lane.

That independence extends beyond records. Starr co-produced and hosted the 2016 documentary Play Your Gender, which examined the severe underrepresentation of women in music production, and she later wrote the score for Edge of the Knife, the first feature film made entirely in the Haida language. After a 2015 traffic collision left her with a brain injury, Feed the Fire in 2018 turned recovery, technology and disconnection into part of the album's subject matter. Across music, film, writing and visual art, Starr's significance lies in the way she has treated authorship, genre freedom and cultural identity as inseparable parts of the same creative practice.
