Northern Dial Artist Profile

Serena Ryder

Peterborough, Ontario, Canada

Raised near Peterborough, Ontario, Serena Ryder developed from a roots-oriented singer-songwriter into one of Canada's most durable crossover voices. Early records established her in folk and acoustic music, while If Your Memory Serves You Well and Is It O.K. widened her national audience. Her powerful, flexible voice became the constant as she moved between folk, rock, soul and pop rather than settling into a single format.

The major commercial turn came with Harmony in 2012. "Stompa" and "What I Wouldn't Do" brought Ryder to a much larger radio audience, and the record helped drive a dominant 2014 JUNO year in which she won Artist of the Year and Songwriter of the Year. Across her career she has accumulated seven JUNO wins, placing her among the more decorated Canadian artists of her generation. Later releases including Utopia continued her pop-rock expansion without erasing the roots foundation underneath it.

Ryder's 2021 album The Art of Falling Apart brought mental health, recovery and self-examination closer to the centre of her writing and won the 2022 JUNO for Adult Contemporary Album of the Year. Her career matters in Canadian music because it connects several ecosystems that are often treated separately: the singer-songwriter circuit, roots music, commercial rock and adult pop. Rather than a single breakthrough defining her, Ryder has repeatedly reshaped the setting around a voice that remains immediately recognizable.
