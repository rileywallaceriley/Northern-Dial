Treble Charger

Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, Canada

Treble Charger formed in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, when Greig Nori, Bill Priddle and Rosie Martin began recording the melodic indie-rock songs that became the independently released 1994 album NC17. After partnering with Hamilton label Sonic Unyon, the record became a major Canadian indie success and "Red" earned heavy college-radio and MuchMusic exposure. The band's early identity was rooted in the 1990s Canadian alternative underground, with power-pop melody and guitar noise arriving before the cleaner pop-punk sound that would later define its commercial peak.

After the major-label album Maybe It's Me, Treble Charger shifted dramatically on Wide Awake Bored. "American Psycho" became a Canadian hit and pushed the album to gold status, while "Brand New Low" reinforced the band's new, faster pop-punk direction. That transition placed Treble Charger directly between two eras of Canadian guitar music: the Sonic Unyon-centered indie network of the 1990s and the internationally successful pop-punk wave that followed. The band toured Summersault in 2000 alongside Our Lady Peace, Foo Fighters and the Smashing Pumpkins before releasing Detox in 2002.

Treble Charger's wider influence is inseparable from its connection to Sum 41. Nori managed and produced the Ajax band during its rise, and his work around that scene helped connect Treble Charger's stylistic pivot to the commercial explosion of Canadian pop-punk in the early 2000s. That relationship has become more complicated in later historical accounts, including serious allegations made by Deryck Whibley that Nori has denied. Musically, however, Treble Charger remains an important hinge in Canadian rock history: a band that began as an independent alternative act and helped make the vocabulary of pop-punk feel native to Canadian mainstream radio.
