Céline Dion

Charlemagne, Québec, Canada

Born in Charlemagne, Québec, Céline Dion began as a francophone teen star under the guidance of manager René Angélil, releasing her first records in the early 1980s and building a substantial career in Québec and France before much of English-speaking North America knew her name. Winning the 1988 Eurovision Song Contest for Switzerland accelerated her international rise, but the decisive crossover came with 1990's Unison and the Top 10 U.S. single "Where Does My Heart Beat Now." Her duet with Peabo Bryson on "Beauty and the Beast" won her first GRAMMY, and "The Power of Love" became her first Billboard Hot 100 No. 1, establishing the scale of the English-language career that followed without ending her work in French.

Dion's 1990s run placed her among the defining global pop vocalists of the decade. The Colour of My Love, Falling into You and Let's Talk About Love produced an extraordinary sequence of international hits, including "Because You Loved Me," "It's All Coming Back to Me Now" and "My Heart Will Go On." Falling into You won the GRAMMY for Album of the Year, while "My Heart Will Go On," written for Titanic, won Record of the Year and became inseparable from both the film and Dion's public identity. Her command of large-scale balladry, technical precision and dramatic phrasing made her a reference point for generations of pop singers, while her ability to move between French- and English-language markets gave a Québec artist unusual global visibility. She has won five GRAMMY Awards and ranks among the most decorated artists in JUNO history.

Her career also changed the economics and expectations of live pop performance. Beginning with A New Day... in 2003, Dion's Las Vegas residencies helped transform the residency from something associated mainly with legacy entertainment into a viable centrepiece for a contemporary superstar's career. Across two Colosseum residencies, she performed 1,141 shows for more than 4.5 million people before closing the second run in 2019, the same year she released Courage. Dion's importance to Canadian music rests not only on record sales or vocal reputation, but on the route she built from a large francophone domestic career to sustained international superstardom while remaining visibly identified with Québec.
