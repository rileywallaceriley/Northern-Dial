Northern Dial Artist Profile

Drake

Toronto, Ontario, Canada

Toronto-born rapper, singer, songwriter and actor Aubrey Drake Graham moved from a teenage role on Degrassi: The Next Generation into one of the defining careers of the streaming era. His early mixtapes built momentum around a style that treated rapping and singing as parts of the same emotional vocabulary, and 2009's “Best I Ever Had” gave him his first U.S. Top 10 hit before a studio album had arrived. His 2010 debut Thank Me Later entered the Billboard 200 at No. 1, while Take Care followed in 2011 with a colder, more spacious sound shaped in part by Toronto collaborators including Noah “40” Shebib and The Weeknd. Take Care won the GRAMMY for Best Rap Album and became a foundation for the introspective rap and R&B hybrid that would dominate much of the next decade.

Drake kept widening that template rather than settling into one lane. Nothing Was the Same sharpened the autobiographical writing, Views turned Toronto itself into the organizing idea for a blockbuster album and produced the global hit “One Dance,” and Scorpion pushed his chart scale further with “God's Plan,” “Nice for What” and “In My Feelings.” Across later projects he continued moving among rap, R&B, dancehall, Afrobeats and pop while treating frequent releases, guest appearances and streaming-era immediacy as part of the work. Through the 2026 GRAMMY Awards, the Recording Academy credits him with five wins and 56 nominations, reflecting a catalogue that has remained commercially and institutionally visible across multiple phases of contemporary hip-hop.

His Canadian significance extends beyond sales and awards. Drake repeatedly foregrounded Toronto in album art, lyrics, slang, videos and branding at a moment when globally successful Canadian artists often minimized local identity. OVO Sound and his long collaboration with 40 helped turn a specific Toronto rap-and-R&B atmosphere into an internationally recognizable aesthetic, while his willingness to place melody, vulnerability and regional influences inside mainstream rap accelerated changes already underway in the genre. The result is a complicated but undeniable legacy: Drake did not create Toronto's hip-hop culture, but his scale made the city impossible to treat as peripheral to 21st-century rap and R&B.
