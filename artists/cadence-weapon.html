Northern Dial Artist Profile

cadence weapon

Edmonton, Alberta, Canada

Edmonton-born Rollie Pemberton grew up around hip-hop through his father, pioneering radio DJ Teddy Pemberton, while also absorbing the city's experimental electronic scene. As Cadence Weapon, he brought those worlds together on the 2005 independent version of Breaking Kayfabe and its wider 2006 release, pairing tightly written rap with abrasive, synthetic production at a time when Canadian hip-hop was still often framed through Toronto. The record began a career defined by regional perspective and a willingness to treat electronic music as part of rap's vocabulary rather than a separate scene.

Hope in Dirt City expanded the sound with live instrumentation, and later records continued to shift between club music, experimental production and social observation. Parallel World became the defining statement of that approach. Released in 2021, the album examined Black life, surveillance, displacement, policing and urban inequality through what Pemberton described as a journalistic lens. It won the Polaris Music Prize after earlier Cadence Weapon records had already reached the prize's shortlist. Rollercoaster, released in 2024, turned that critical attention toward platforms, algorithms and the strange economics of online culture.

Pemberton's significance extends beyond recording. He served as Edmonton's poet laureate from 2009 to 2011 and later published the memoir Bedroom Rapper, making writing about place and culture a parallel part of his practice. In Canadian hip-hop, Cadence Weapon is important because he offers a different map of the genre: Edmonton roots, Montréal and Toronto connections, experimental electronic production and sustained criticism of how technology and cities shape everyday life.
