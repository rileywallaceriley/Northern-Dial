Julie Doiron

Moncton, New Brunswick, Canada

Moncton-born Acadian singer-songwriter Julie Doiron entered Canadian indie-rock history as a teenager in Eric's Trip, playing guitar and then bass as the band developed its mix of melody, distortion and lo-fi intimacy. Formed in New Brunswick in 1990, Eric's Trip became the first Canadian band signed to Seattle's Sub Pop Records, with albums including Love Tara helping establish an Atlantic Canadian branch of the 1990s indie-rock underground. Before the group first split in 1996, Doiron had already begun releasing quieter solo material under the name Broken Girl; she subsequently continued under her own name, carrying the emotional directness of that scene into an increasingly personal songwriting catalogue.

Her solo career quickly proved that the post-Eric's Trip work was more than a side project. Julie Doiron and the Wooden Stars, recorded with the Ottawa band Wooden Stars, won the 2000 JUNO Award for Alternative Album of the Year. She followed with records that moved between fragile folk, noisy guitar music and bilingual songwriting, including the French-language Désormais. The 2007 album Woke Myself Up reunited her with former Eric's Trip bandmates and was shortlisted for the inaugural Polaris Music Prize, while I Can Wonder What You Did with Your Day and So Many Days extended a catalogue built around small observations, domestic detail and songs that can move from near-whisper to full-band distortion without losing their intimacy.

Doiron's importance also comes from the network around that catalogue. She has collaborated with artists including Gordon Downie, the Tragically Hip, Mount Eerie and Okkervil River, linking Atlantic Canadian indie rock to wider folk, experimental and singer-songwriter communities. After a long gap between solo albums, 2021's I Thought of You returned to a louder band setting and demonstrated how naturally her early indie-rock instincts still fit the plainspoken writing she developed over decades. That continuity makes Doiron a connective figure in Canadian independent music: part of a landmark Sub Pop band, a JUNO-winning solo artist and a songwriter whose understated influence extends well beyond one scene or era.
