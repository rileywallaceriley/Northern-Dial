# Northern Dial Grant Readiness Package

## Applicant structure
Northern Dial is currently an independent, Riley-led Canadian music project. It is not currently represented as an incorporated nonprofit. Applications should use Riley Wallace as the lead applicant where the program permits individual artists, arts workers or independent project leads. Programs requiring an eligible organization should be approached later through incorporation or an eligible partner rather than misrepresenting current status.

## Core mandate
**Discovery. Documentation. Dissemination.** Northern Dial helps listeners find Canadian music, adds source-backed context around artists and creates additional routes for Canadian music to reach audiences.

## Current public assets
- 24/7 Canadian radio stream
- Curated Canadian Artist Library
- Northern Dial Discovery
- Editorial coverage and requests
- Rights, Sources and Corrections policy
- English/French institutional pages
- Accessibility commitment and automated checks
- Bilingual pilot audience survey

## Project team model
**Project lead, editorial and product: Riley Wallace.** Owns mandate, editorial standards, grant delivery, product direction, research and reporting.

**Development collaborator function.** Technical implementation, data workflows, web development and platform reliability. Filled by independent collaborator(s) as required.

**Partnerships and audience-development collaborator function.** Outreach, sponsorship/sales support, community relationships, artist/venue coordination and audience growth. Filled as required.

**Design and curation collaborator function.** Visual design, experience design, music/program curation and public-facing presentation. Filled as required.

Do not convert these functions into named staff or permanent employees in an application unless those people have actually committed to the project.

## Objectives
1. Increase meaningful discovery of Canadian artists, especially independent, emerging, regional and under-documented artists.
2. Improve the quality and provenance of public artist information.
3. Develop bilingual and accessible paths into the project.
4. Build evidence of audience use and cultural value.
5. Create fundable next-stage programs without overstating current organizational capacity.

## Outputs
- Expanded and verified artist profiles
- Discovery and recommendation features
- Bilingual public project information
- Rights/provenance and correction process
- Accessibility testing process
- Audience research dataset
- Future Northern Dial Sessions pilot
- Future Canadian Music Archive and Atlas prototype

## Intended outcomes
- More listeners discover Canadian artists they did not previously know.
- Artists gain additional discovery and contextual documentation pathways.
- Researchers, journalists and music communities gain a more useful source-backed starting point.
- Northern Dial develops measurable evidence for future funding and partnerships.

## Audiences
Primary: Canadian music listeners seeking discovery outside mainstream algorithms.
Secondary: Canadian artists, regional music communities, researchers, journalists, curators and cultural organizations.

## Evaluation framework
Track unique listeners, returning listeners, artist requests, Discovery uses, outbound artist-link clicks, submissions, survey responses, percentage of survey respondents reporting a new artist discovery, profiles completed, corrections processed, regional representation and accessibility issues found/resolved.

## Accessibility approach
Target WCAG 2.2 AA practices, keyboard usability, semantic structure, useful alt text, labelled forms, readable contrast, reduced-motion support, responsive layouts and captions/transcripts for future media. Automated checks are a floor, not certification; periodic manual keyboard and screen-reader review is still required.

## Bilingual approach
Core institutional pages are being provided in English and French. Existing artist profiles and editorial articles remain in their original/source language until genuinely translated. Future funded projects should budget for professional translation and comparative editing where required.

## Rights and provenance
Keep source references with artist metadata where practical. Separate open-source code licensing from third-party images, recordings, compositions and artist-owned materials. Provide a correction/takedown route and do not ask rights holders to post sensitive documents publicly.

## Illustrative delivery phases
**Phase 1: Research and design (1-2 months).** Audience research, partner conversations, scope, rights review and accessibility plan.
**Phase 2: Build/pilot (3-5 months).** Content production, development, bilingual work, testing and limited launch.
**Phase 3: Public launch (1 month).** Promotion, partner activation and public release.
**Phase 4: Evaluation (1 month).** Analyze use, artist/audience feedback, accessibility findings and next-stage recommendations.

## Budget framework
Build grant budgets from real quotes. Common lines: artist fees, project management/editorial, development, design/curation, translation and comparative editing, accessibility testing, rights/licensing, documentation/production, outreach/marketing and contingency where permitted. Track Riley's in-kind hours separately when a program allows in-kind contributions.

## Risks and mitigations
- **Eligibility mismatch:** apply only through a permitted applicant type or eligible partner.
- **Founder concentration:** scope projects realistically and budget collaborator functions.
- **Rights uncertainty:** use source tracking, permission workflows and takedown procedures.
- **Incomplete bilingual content:** translate defined project deliverables professionally rather than claiming the entire legacy catalogue is bilingual.
- **Accessibility gaps:** combine automated checks with manual testing and specialist review on funded projects.
- **Weak evidence:** run the survey and instrument product analytics before major applications.

## Evidence to collect next
Listener/visitor analytics, Discovery usage, artist-request counts, survey results, representative artist/user testimonials, collaborator commitments, partner letters and project-specific cost quotes.
