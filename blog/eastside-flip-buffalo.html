Eastside Flip, the Buffalo Figure Tied to Griselda, Dies at 36

September 6, 2026

Eastside Flip, the Buffalo community figure whose presence became closely tied to Westside Gunn and the Griselda world, has died after a reported hit-and-run in his hometown.

According to local reporting, Flip was struck by a vehicle on Buffalo’s Bailey Avenue bridge late Thursday night. Police said the driver left the scene. Flip was taken to Erie County Medical Center, where he later died from his injuries. Local reports identified him as 36-year-old Zaire Allison.

The circumstances were still developing as reports appeared on September 4 and 5. A post from Flip’s personal Instagram account also described his death as a hit-and-run, while no arrest had been publicly confirmed at the time of publication.

A Buffalo presence bigger than the artwork

Flip was not a conventional recording artist or a signed member of Griselda Records. He was a recognizable East Buffalo presence whose personality, local history and relationship with Westside Gunn carried into the label’s visual language.

That connection was most visibly preserved on the cover of Gunn’s 2022 album 10, which placed Flip at the forefront of a release that marked a major moment in Gunn’s catalogue. His appearances made him familiar to Griselda fans far beyond Buffalo, but the meaning of those images began in a personal relationship rather than a marketing strategy.

AllHipHop reported that Flip also appeared in Griselda-related videos and on Gunn’s later work, including the 2025 album 12. Gunn publicly mourned his friend after Flip’s death, underscoring the bond behind their creative association.

Context matters

Flip had previously survived an overdose in late 2022. That was a separate incident. At the time, Gunn spoke publicly about Flip being revived after being taken to hospital and pushed back against people discussing his friend without understanding his circumstances.

For the Griselda audience, Flip was never simply a recurring face on promotional material. He represented a distinctly Buffalo perspective: local, charismatic and inseparable from the community that helped shape the label’s rise. His presence gave the imagery a sense of place that could not be manufactured after the fact.

There is still much that has not been established publicly about the crash. For now, the clearest account is also the simplest: Buffalo lost one of its recognizable figures, and a relationship that had connected a neighbourhood presence to one of rap’s most distinctive modern movements has been cut short.
