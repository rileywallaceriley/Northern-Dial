25 Canadian Records Worth Hearing This November

By Northern Dial Staff · November 13, 2026

Twenty-five records from across the Canadian music scene. No rankings. Just some good places to start digging.

November has been busy. Some of these records barely registered in the bigger roundups. Others haven't travelled far beyond their own scenes. There's Métis fiddling recovered from family videotapes, a Dene duo from Yellowknife, Acadian prog sung in Chiac, Winnipeg hardcore, Montreal trad, and a Toronto band finishing a zodiac cycle it started two decades ago.

We've grouped them by release week. Beyond that, there's no particular order.

Hear the playlist: Two songs from almost every record below, sequenced to play from start to finish. (Fucked Up's record isn't on Spotify, so we've linked to Bandcamp instead.)

Album · November 4

Benjamin Russell: Elemental

Montreal singer-songwriter Benjamin Russell wrote and recorded these ten tracks while undergoing diagnosis and treatment for pancreatic cancer. Pop, punk, opera, jazz, folk and ambient music all find their way in. It's a lot of ground to cover, but Russell doesn't seem interested in staying in one place.

Listen to the release

Album · November 4

Brianna Lizotte: Blood Memory

Seven ancestral fiddle tunes, recovered from family VHS tapes, sit alongside eight new compositions on Brianna Lizotte's third album. The Edmonton Métis fiddler was nominated for Traditional Indigenous Artist or Group of the Year at the 2025 JUNO Awards for Winston & I. Here, she's working directly with the music preserved in her own family archive.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Comeback Kid: Chosen Hell

More than twenty years into their career, Winnipeg hardcore veterans Comeback Kid are back with their eighth studio album. Chosen Hell delivers eleven tracks on Dine Alone in Canada. No reinvention required.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

DijahSB: Why Die When You Can Dance?

DijahSB's new independent full-length brings Toronto hip-hop into the mix. The title asks a pretty good question. The answer, at least here, is to keep dancing. It's a record about defiance, made on their own terms.

Listen to the release · Northern Dial artist profile

Album · November 6

Hayden: The Recent Past

Hayden founded Hardwood Records in 1994. He's revived the label for The Recent Past, another quiet entry in the veteran Toronto songwriter's catalogue. Thirty years in, he still knows how to make understatement count.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Taylor Ashton: Charades

Toronto songwriter Taylor Ashton returns with Charades, a banjo-led folk record on Birthday Cake Records. It takes its time. That's part of the appeal.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Yoo Doo Right: Build and Rest by the Shepherd's Light

Montreal psych-rock outfit Yoo Doo Right has never been in much of a hurry. Build and Rest by the Shepherd's Light, out on Mothland, gives the music room to stretch. Turn it up and settle in.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Lammping: So What, What?

Lammping are three records into a four-part album cycle that began with Never Never and continued with Risky. So What, What? is also the Toronto duo's fifth full-length overall. Released through We Are Busy Bodies, it carries on the series' restless, collage-like approach.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Tennyson King: 100 Cities

A hundred cities on the road gave Toronto-based songwriter Tennyson King the title for 100 Cities. His indie folk incorporates traditional Chinese instrumentation, bringing another set of sounds and influences to a record shaped by touring.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Janky Bungag: Hangin' Out & Hangin' On

There's a pretty big difference between hanging out and hanging on. Vancouver alt-country flatpicker Janky Bungag gets both into the title of Hangin' Out & Hangin' On, released through Royal Mountain Records. The songs find plenty to work with between those two states.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Laura Niquay: Nir Wactenama

Atikamekw singer-songwriter Laura Niquay returns with her third studio album, Nir Wactenama. Sung in her own language and rooted in blues rock, the record arrives through Musique Nomade. It's worth making time for.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

young friend: New Entertainment

Vancouver's Drew Tarves, who records as young friend, went fully analog for his sophomore album. Working with producer David Marinelli, he recorded the twelve songs live off the floor. New Entertainment arrives on Nettwerk, another step forward for the JUNO-nominated songwriter.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Sechile Sedare: Sechile Sedare

Leela Gilday and Jay Gilday have been singing together their whole lives. The Dene siblings from Yellowknife make their full-length debut as Sechile Sedare with ten tracks recorded in Edmonton. Family, land and water run through the songs, with the pair's lifelong musical connection at the centre.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Les Moontunes: Cyborg Slayer

A sci-fi concept album sung entirely in Chiac? Moncton's Les Moontunes have made one. The seven-piece band's third studio album is also their first entirely in the French-based Acadian vernacular. Cyborg Slayer brings thirteen tracks of prog rock to Acadian Embassy.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

clay pigeon: Cash Out in Kid Gloves

Peterborough-born James Clayton returns with the second album from his Montreal project clay pigeon. Cash Out in Kid Gloves is eight tracks of self-released alt-country and indie folk.

The title is Kid Gloves, by the way. Some listings have it as "Kids Gloves," but the artist uses the singular.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

É.T.É: Aronde

Élisabeth Moquin, Thierry Clouette and Élisabeth Giroux make up Québec trad trio É.T.É. Their approach to traditional music is lively and inventive, with fiddles and voices driving the arrangements. Aronde also caught the attention of Le Devoir, which reviewed the record on release.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Jacob Do: First Chorus

Edmonton tenor saxophonist Jacob Do makes his full-length debut with First Chorus, an eight-track release on Bent River Records. Canadian jazz veteran PJ Perry makes a guest appearance. Not bad company for a debut.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Shiloh Lindsey: The Western Veil

It's been almost a decade since Vancouver singer Shiloh Lindsey released a full-length. The Western Veil follows her recovery from a 2024 motorcycle accident, an experience that informed the writing. The result is ten tracks of alt-country and Canadiana, with plenty of life behind them.

Listen to the release

Album · November 6

Nova Spei: Trinitas

Trois-Rivières metal band Nova Spei introduces a new frontwoman, Emmanuelle Desbiens-Dubois, on its third full-length. Francis Perron, Voivod's longtime engineer, handles production. Trinitas packs thirteen tracks of French-language modern metal onto a release through Bam&Co-Heavy.

Listen to the release

Album · November 13

renforshort: Hotel 13

Toronto alt-pop singer renforshort returns with her sophomore record, Hotel 13, and a seven-city Canadian tour already announced for February 2027. Across fourteen tracks, the hotel becomes a place to step outside ordinary life and say things that might otherwise go unsaid.

Listen to the release

Album · November 13

Land of Talk: LASTS

Elizabeth Powell recorded LASTS in rural Harrington, Quebec. The eight-track Land of Talk record arrives through Saddle Creek and Next Door Records. There's something fitting about Powell making a record in a cabin, given how much space and intimacy have always mattered to the band's music.

Listen to the release

Album · November 13

Chastity: I Built This Prison Myself

Whitby's Chastity returns to Dine Alone with I Built This Prison Myself. The ten-track punk record takes on confinement, frustration and the struggle to get free. The title doesn't leave much room for misunderstanding.

Listen to the release · Northern Dial artist profile

Album · November 13

Fog Lake: Hollowbody

Aaron Powell has been making music as Fog Lake out of Glovertown, Newfoundland. Hollowbody adds ten tracks to the project's catalogue of intimate, lo-fi dream pop. Quiet, hazy and a little haunted, it's the sort of record that rewards a late-night listen.

Listen to the release

Album · November 13

Fucked Up: Grass Can Move Stones Part 3: Year of the Rooster

Fucked Up's long-running Zodiac series reaches its conclusion with Grass Can Move Stones Part 3: Year of the Rooster. The Toronto band has been working on the cycle alongside its wider career, and now it's finally complete.

This one slipped past the big November roundups. It's also the only record here missing from our Spotify playlist. Head to Bandcamp for the full release.

Listen to the release

Album · November 13

Shaina Hayes: Take the Case of a Rose

Raised in Shigawake in the Gaspésie, Montreal singer Shaina Hayes releases her third album through Bonsound. Take the Case of a Rose has ten songs on its digital edition, while the CD and LP include an additional three-track suite called SANCTUM. A little something extra for anyone still buying physical records.

Listen to the release

One more is still to come: Bodywash's Laughing at Noise, out November 20. We'll add it here when it's out.

For now, pick a name you don't recognize. Follow a label. See where the music takes you. There's plenty here to get lost in.
