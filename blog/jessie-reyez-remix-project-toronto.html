Jessie Reyez Shines Light on the Remix Project During Tour Finale

November 25, 2026

Jessie Reyez closed her A Little Vengeance Tour at home on Tuesday, November 24, at the Coca-Cola Coliseum in Toronto. It was the last date of the tour, and she told the room so herself, holding a bouquet of blue and orange roses: this would be her last show "for a long, long time."

She did not close it alone. The bill was a Toronto story in itself. Seven openers, all from the city, Faiza, Adria Kain, Kalisway, Devontée, Russell!, Savannah Ré, and Kaiyel, with Rich Kidd hosting and DJing the warm-up set. Three of the seven came up through the Remix Project, the same program Reyez came through before she was headlining arenas.

The Remix Project is a Toronto youth arts incubator for creatives aged 15 to 22, founded in 2006 by Gavin Sheppard, Drex Jancar, and Kehinde Bah. It grew out of Inner City Visions, a drop-in program Sheppard started in 2000, and runs three academies, Recording Arts, Creative Arts, and Business, taking about 50 students a semester from roughly 250 applicants. Its point, in Sheppard's words, is to "level the playing field" for young creatives the usual routes overlook. Its alumni include WondaGurl, FrancisGotHeat, Daniel Daley, and Mustafa the Poet, and its mentors have included Noah "40" Shebib and Boi-1da.

The three alumni on Tuesday's bill each came through it a different way. Adria Kain came up through Remix alongside Daley and Reyez herself. Savannah Ré went through the Recording Arts program and credits the Remix community for how she learned to work. Kalisway joined at 18 or 19 to train as an audio engineer, until Rich Kidd heard her arranging and songwriting and pushed her to step out as an artist instead.

Kidd's presence closed the loop. He is a Remix Project instructor, the one who redirected Kalisway, and on Tuesday he hosted the night and DJed the warm-up set. Reyez named her openers from the stage, thanking them by first name before the tears caught up with her.

She used the moment for something else too. "Me and you are exactly the f**king same. We bleed the same. We breathe the same," she told the crowd, calling for "more f**king unity at all times, especially in this city," and asking them to end the night "on a f**king high note." She was, she said, "so f**king grateful and so f**king happy to finish the tour at home."

A homecoming is one thing. A farewell is another. Reyez closed the tour the way she started it: inside the community that built her, with the program that raised her, its instructor on the mic, and three of its graduates on the stage.
