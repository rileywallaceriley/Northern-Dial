5 New Canadian Releases to Bump This Weekend

A new Mattmac album, a strange Calgary debut, two records built far from the usual Canadian music centres and the return of a Nova Scotia rap group after three decades.

Northern Dial artwork

This weekend's picks don't have much in common beyond a Canadian postal code. That's the point. The five releases below move through hip-hop, electronic pop, guitar music, prairie country and an unexpected piece of East Coast rap history.

All five artists are now in the Northern Dial library. Follow the profile links below to hear where they fit in the wider station.

Mattmac — Smoke Signals

Mattmac's third album brings Garden Hill First Nation into focus without treating home like scenery. Smoke Signals was made in close collaboration with Atlanta artist and producer QuestionATL, connecting the Southern rap Mattmac grew up hearing with memories of northern Manitoba, displacement and the people who shaped him. It's a personal record with enough low-end to work outside the headphones.

CG Tears — CG Tears

Calgary trio Clinton St. John, Morgan Greenwood and Chad VanGaalen took the long route to a debut. The self-titled CG Tears folds synths, odd rhythmic turns and damaged-pop instincts into songs that keep changing shape without losing the hook. “I'm Falling Up” is a good entry point.

Watch “I'm Falling Up” on YouTube

Jon McKiel — Gold Horatio

Jon McKiel recorded Gold Horatio with Jay Crocker in Baie Verte and Sackville, New Brunswick. The record has the closeness of a home recording without feeling unfinished. Guitar songs bend at the edges, production choices arrive a little sideways and McKiel leaves enough space for the weird details to stick.

Explore Jon McKiel's Northern Dial profile →

Hear “For Shadow” on YouTube

Starpainter — Cayenne Flowerbed

Lethbridge's Starpainter released Cayenne Flowerbed on September 9, giving the weekend a prairie-country record that doesn't need to announce itself as one. Joel Stretch's songs sit comfortably beside slide guitar, acoustic strum and Chris Dadge's loose, detailed production. “Rabbits” and the title track are both good doors in.

Explore Starpainter's Northern Dial profile →

Hear Starpainter on YouTube

Hip Club Groove — “Dead Words”

This one needs an asterisk: Bassments of Badmen 4 is a Hand'Solo Records compilation, not a Hip Club Groove album. What makes it essential this weekend is “Dead Words,” the Nova Scotia group's first new song in 30 years. Cheklove Shakil and MacKenzie are back on the mic with Moves handling production and cuts. Three decades later, the chemistry still sounds like it belongs to them.
