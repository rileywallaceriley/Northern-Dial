If You Like Kaytranada, Listen to These 5 Canadian Artists

September 10, 2026

Kaytranada sits at a useful crossroads in Canadian music: Montréal beat culture, house, funk, R&B, hip-hop and Caribbean rhythm all meet somewhere inside his records. So rather than find five artists who simply sound like him, we followed five different roads outward.

The point is not to find five Kaytranada copies. It is to figure out what part of his music pulls you in, then use that as a doorway into somebody else.

The obvious bonus pick: Lou Phelps. Kaytranada's younger brother has years of shared musical DNA, collaborations and production credits with him. If you have somehow skipped Lou Phelps, start there. Then go wider with the five artists below.

1. Jacques Greene

Start with Jacques Greene if the part of Kaytranada you love most is the point where R&B feeling meets club music. The Montréal producer has spent years turning chopped vocals, house structures and small melodic details into records that feel emotional without losing their physical pull.

There is also a particularly good reason to catch up with him now. Greene's third solo album, jg, arrives October 16, and he has described it as his first true club album. The early singles strip away some of the haze around his older work and push the drums forward. That makes "Presence" an ideal entry point for a Kaytranada listener in 2026: familiar territory, but a very different producer's instincts.

The connection: House and R&B are the shared language. Greene leans further into emotional club architecture and UK dance music.

Start with: “Presence” · Explore Jacques Greene on Northern Dial

2. BAMBII

BAMBII takes the Caribbean-club side of this conversation somewhere harder and faster. The Toronto producer and DJ folds her Jamaican roots into jungle, dancehall, garage and electronic music, with the city's multicultural club ecosystem sitting underneath all of it.

“One Touch” is the cleanest starting point. The track runs on jungle velocity, dancehall phrasing and a huge low end rather than Kaytranada's signature pocket, but the connection is deeper than a drum pattern. Both artists treat Caribbean music as living material that can move naturally through electronic and North American club spaces. If Kaytranada makes you want to move, BAMBII pushes that impulse closer to the rave.

The connection: Caribbean rhythmic lineage, bass-heavy club music and a refusal to keep dancehall, R&B and electronic music in separate boxes.

Start with: “One Touch” · Listen on Bandcamp

3. Rochelle Jordan

Rochelle Jordan is not just Kaytranada-adjacent. Their catalogues actually intersect. Kaytranada remixed her song “All Along,” then brought Jordan into his own world on “Lover/Friend” and “Spit It Out.” The pairing works because Jordan's music was already living comfortably between futuristic R&B, house, UK garage, electronic pop and hip-hop.

That makes “All Along” the right place to begin. Even before the Kaytranada remix, the original had the bounce and house-minded percussion that make Jordan such a natural fit for dance music without reducing her to a club vocalist. Follow it into Play With the Changes, then circle back to the Kaytranada collaborations and you can hear exactly why the chemistry works.

The connection: This one is literal. Jordan and Kaytranada are recurring collaborators, but her own catalogue gives the same R&B-meets-dance instinct a vocalist's perspective.

Start with: “All Along” · Explore Rochelle Jordan on Northern Dial

4. Planet Giza

Planet Giza opens the hip-hop door. The Montréal trio builds around rap first, but funk, jazz, soul and producer-driven groove are baked into the group's records. That alone makes them an easy next stop for anyone who comes to Kaytranada through his work with rappers.

Then there is “Sometimes,” from the 2023 album Ready When You Are. Kaytranada is one of the track's credited producers alongside Planet Giza, JMF and Da-P, so the connection is not theoretical. More importantly, the song lets you hear his rhythmic language inside Planet Giza's own world rather than the other way around. From there, the rest of the album moves further into the trio's jazzier, funkier version of Montréal hip-hop.

The connection: Montréal's beat scene, funk-minded hip-hop and an actual Kaytranada co-production provide the bridge.

Start with: “Sometimes” featuring Sun · Explore Planet Giza on Northern Dial

5. KALLITECHNIS

KALLITECHNIS makes the Montréal family tree visible. Before she had a substantial catalogue of her own, her voice appeared on Lou Phelps' 2017 single “Average,” produced by Kaytranada. Planet Giza's Rami B had helped connect her to the city's music community, which means several of the threads in this list meet in the same place.

Nearly a decade later, she is not simply a footnote in somebody else's story. Her 2026 project kosmos, made with mango heist, moves directly into house while keeping her R&B and soul identity intact. The title track is the better introduction for this list because it shows where that Montréal connection can lead when an artist follows her own route into dance music.

The connection: A direct Kaytranada history, Montréal R&B roots and a current move into house make KALLITECHNIS the family-tree pick.

Start with: “kosmos” · Listen on Apple Music

Kaytranada is the starting point here, not the destination. Jacques Greene takes you deeper into Montréal club music. BAMBII follows the Caribbean current into jungle and dancehall. Rochelle Jordan puts R&B vocals at the centre. Planet Giza opens the hip-hop lane. KALLITECHNIS shows how tightly connected the Montréal ecosystem can be.

That is the idea behind If You Like...: start with a name you know, follow one part of the sound, and keep moving.
