Mattmac’s Smoke Signals Carries Home, Distance and Memory

On his third album, the Garden Hill First Nation artist connects the rap records that raised him with the people and places that still shape his work.

Northern Dial artwork

Mattmac has spent the last several years building a career that reaches well beyond Garden Hill First Nation, but Smoke Signals keeps circling back there.

The Anisininew rapper, singer and producer was born blind and started making music young, first singing in his community gospel choir and later teaching himself production, piano and guitar. His path widened through the N'we Jinan program and a run of independent releases, but the story on Smoke Signals is less about arrival than memory.

Atlanta artist and producer QuestionATL is central to the album. The partnership gives Smoke Signals a direct line to the Southern rap Mattmac grew up hearing while leaving enough room for his own melodic instincts. The connection works because it doesn't feel like a borrowed aesthetic. Mattmac has talked openly about those records as part of his early listening life, and the album treats that influence as something lived with rather than discovered later.

“I Remember When,” one of the clearest examples of that chemistry, uses recollection as the engine. The song looks backward without freezing the past in place. The people, sounds and distances in Mattmac's story keep moving with him.

Place matters throughout Mattmac's work. Garden Hill is a fly-in community in northern Manitoba, and distance is not an abstract idea there. Travel, access and separation have a practical weight. That gives the album's memories extra texture: home can be close emotionally while remaining difficult to reach physically.

Smoke Signals doesn't flatten that experience into a lesson for listeners outside the community. Mattmac writes from inside it. Family, friendships, displacement and ambition show up as parts of the same life instead of separate themes waiting to be explained.

The biggest shift is confidence in how much the record can hold at once. Mattmac can make something personal without turning down the drums, and he can draw from Atlanta without losing Manitoba. The album's 14 tracks give him room to move between reflection and momentum rather than choosing one lane.

That range is what makes Smoke Signals feel like a new chapter. Mattmac isn't introducing himself anymore. He's working with the pieces already on the table and deciding how they fit together.
