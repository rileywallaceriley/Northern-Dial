7 Canadian Records You Might Have Missed in October

By Northern Dial Staff · November 1, 2026

Seven records from seven different corners of the Canadian music scene. Not a ranking, just a few good places to keep digging.

The busiest release weeks are easy to scan and harder to remember. October brought new music from familiar names and a long list of independent artists across Canada. These seven records offer a route through some of the month’s other corners. Consider this a browsing list, in no particular order.

Album · October 16

Sophia Stel: the greatest album of all time

The Vancouver artist’s full-length debut arrives through A24 Music after the EP How to Win at Solitaire. Its twelve tracks include “Molly In The Club,” “I Do” and “Better Believe It.” The deliberately oversized title fits an artist whose songs are willing to put big, pointed statements in the foreground.

Debut album · October 2

Virgo Rising: Wishbone

Winnipeg’s Virgo Rising wrote, arranged, performed and produced the record themselves. Emily Sinclair, Jenna Wittmann, Lauren Wittmann and Isaac Tate recorded at No Fun Club and Basement Studio. It follows the group’s earlier Sixteenth Sapphire and Vampyre Year EPs.

Album · October 16

Jacques Greene: jg

The Montréal producer builds his third solo album around dance-floor collaboration rather than a single guest cameo. CFCF, Ouri, Jonah Yano, DJ BORING and umru appear on an eleven-track record mixed by Pearson Sound and mastered by Joker.

Listen to the release · Northern Dial artist profile

Album · October 23

Art d’Ecco: Scheele’s Green

Art d’Ecco made this record with Eric Shea at Vancouver’s Hipposonic Studios. “The Death of Indie” opens the release with a sharp look at the technology industry’s appetite for new territory; “Catch 22” and “How Real is Real?” sit further into the tracklist.

Listen to the release

Album · October 23

Boyhood: I’ll Dream InStead

For Caylie Runciman’s latest Boyhood album, the recording process was notably self-contained: she wrote, performed, recorded and engineered all eight tracks, later working with mixer James Bunton. “Loner” and “A Grand Time” point toward another chapter in her long-running Ontario DIY project.

Listen to the release

Album · October 16

Allegories: By Accident, On Purpose

Hamilton’s Allegories, the duo of Adam Bentley and Jordan Mitchell, return with electronic music that began in smaller sketches before being rebuilt into layered compositions. The album adds a more electronic Hamilton sound to a month dominated by larger rock and pop announcements.

Listen on Allegories’ Bandcamp

Album · October 9

Drezus: Nagamo

The Indigenous hip-hop artist released a twelve-track record through Public Records. At 31 minutes, Nagamo offers another entry point into the contemporary Indigenous rap scene, beyond the handful of artists who usually appear on broad national release lists.

Listen to the release · Northern Dial artist profile

The broader October release calendar includes dozens more records. This shortlist is designed for browsing: follow a label, try a new name or move between scenes rather than treating the month as a competition.
