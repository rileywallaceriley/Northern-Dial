Fresh Finds Canada: 6 Artists and the Songs to Hear on Northern Dial

September 2, 2026

Spotify's Fresh Finds Canada playlist is a useful snapshot of the country's independent music ecosystem. Northern Dial's shortcut is even more useful: at the time of publication, every song discussed in this guide is already in our library. You can read about the artists, then hear the music on the station.

The playlist moves across pop, R&B, hip-hop, indie and alternative sounds, so this is not a tidy genre list. It is a set of six distinct Canadian stories—and six good places to begin.

01 · Enola Bédard

Start with “Unavez.” Quebec-born Enola Bédard brings a dancer's sense of movement to her music. She is a dancer, choreographer, singer and creator, and that combination makes her an especially interesting artist to watch: the song is part of a larger performance language rather than a separate soundtrack to it. Her official biography points to original music beginning in 2023, with a debut EP and headline tour planned for 2026.

02 · Essiyas

Start with “used to.” Vancouver's Essiyas works in a darker, more atmospheric corner of contemporary R&B. His own description emphasizes a dark and ethereal sound, while the music draws on R&B, hip-hop and soul. The result is late-night music with enough space around the vocal to let the feeling linger.

03 · Hayley Law

Start with “WET WET.” Hayley Law's music moves between pop, R&B and pop-punk with diaristic songwriting at the centre. Her recent Last Summer project gives this feature a timely entry point, while her independent approach keeps the focus on the songs rather than the celebrity résumé. It is polished, personal and more restless than a standard pop recommendation.

04 · Ada Lea

Start with “the evergreen, the birch.” Montreal's Ada Lea has a knack for turning intimate songwriting into textured arrangements. The playlist track is a collaboration with MEGGO, and her catalogue often leaves room for unexpected electronics, synths and crunchy guitars. She is a natural bridge between indie songwriting and the more exploratory side of Canadian pop.

05 · Gliiico

Start with “Don't Eat Don't Sleep.” Gliiico are a Vancouver-born trio now associated with Tokyo's alternative scene. Their sound brings psychedelic haze, alt-rock and funk into the same room. That Canadian-to-international path is part of the appeal, but the song is the real argument: bright, strange and difficult to leave politely in the background.

06 · Derin Falana

Start with “Good Call.” Toronto rapper Derin Falana builds from melodic flows, introspection and the everyday pressure of trying to move forward. His 2025 return gives the story momentum, and his catalogue places him firmly inside Toronto's evolving hip-hop conversation without reducing the music to geography.

Hear the songs: Listen live on Northern Dial, or browse the song library to explore what has played recently. For the wider playlist context, visit Spotify's Fresh Finds Canada.

These six are only a starting point. The playlist changes, the library keeps moving, and that is the fun part: Canadian music discovery works best as a living conversation rather than a frozen listicle.
