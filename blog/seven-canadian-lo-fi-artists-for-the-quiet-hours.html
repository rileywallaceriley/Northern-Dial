Listening Guide

Seven Canadian Lo-Fi Artists for the Quiet Hours

November 22, 2026

Lo-fi might be the most heard and least chosen music on the internet. It pours out of study playlists, rain videos and all-day streams, usually with nobody's name attached. That anonymity is a waste. Some of the best of it is made in Canada, by people with long catalogues that hold up when you actually sit down with them.

Canada has been building toward this sound for a while. The country's trip-hop generation was making slow, sample-heavy records decades before lo-fi became a playlist category, and we have a whole feature on those Canadian trip-hop classics if you want the family history. This piece is about the current wave.

For the sound in your head when someone says lo-fi

Laffey

If a stranger asked what lo-fi sounds like, you could play them Laffey and end the conversation. The Toronto producer works with soft guitar, plain piano and a lot of open space, and the records keep that promise all the way through: Ten from 2018, Summer Nights, The Moon and the Mind.

Nothing on a Laffey track is showing off. The drums sit back, the melodies repeat until they feel like weather, and a song like "where did the time go," his track with Aso, does its whole job in under three minutes.

Start with: "where did the time go" · Then hear: "After The Rain" and "Forever Changing"

For beats with verses on top

Sarcastic Sounds

Most lo-fi stays instrumental on purpose. Jeremy Fedryk, the Toronto producer and singer behind Sarcastic Sounds, keeps the dusty drums and the sad piano and then writes verses over them. He came up selling compositions online, fell into lo-fi hip-hop, and started finishing the songs himself instead of handing them off.

He collaborates with singers and rappers like Powfu, Rxseboy and Sody, and his production has travelled well beyond the lo-fi world, including a beat on Lil Wayne's Funeral ("Mahogany"). Start with "I Don't Sleep," an early song from 2018 that has been streamed tens of millions of times. The hook sounds like it was written at 4 a.m.

Start with: "I Don't Sleep" · Then hear: "fools (can't help falling in love)" and "Disappointment"

For the jazz running underneath

Berezy

Ottawa-born, Berezy builds chillhop and jazzhop where you can hear the playing, and his grooves have a warmth that sample packs cannot fake. His 2025 album Memoirs is a good place to start.

"Telesto" drifts, "Westerly Wind" leans into the jazz side, and "Jube Jubes," named after the Canadian candy, bounces.

Start with: "Telesto" · Then hear: "Jube Jubes" and "Westerly Wind"

For the dusty end of the dial

bsd.u

Matthew Veselisin, based on Vancouver Island, came up in the SoundCloud era and still makes beats that sound dug up rather than designed: hazy, sample-driven, a little frayed at the edges. He also records under the name monk.

His records pook and lighter came out on Inner Ocean Records, the Calgary label that has quietly documented a lot of this music in Canada. "French Inhale" and "Bebop" are the SoundCloud-era sound, and "Hoodie Weather" might be the most honest title in the whole genre.

Start with: "French Inhale" · Then hear: "Bebop" and "Hoodie Weather"

For the polished end of chillhop

Makzo

Zack Mortimer, who records as Makzo, was born in Canada and now lives in London, England. He records for Chillhop Music, the label most associated with this sound worldwide, and his work sits at its polished end: warm, melodic, carefully finished.

His album Wanderlust (2021) is the fullest statement. Since then the singles have kept coming: "Sycamore," made with Leavv and Beats for Trees, "Saturn," with Lucid Green, and "Amber," a 2026 collaboration with Leavv. Plenty of Canadian talent ends up based somewhere else. The passport still counts.

Start with: "Sycamore" · Then hear: "Amber" and "Saturn"

For watching it happen live

The Kount

Most of the artists here let the music speak and stay out of frame. The Kount does the opposite. Koal Harrison, a Toronto producer and multi-instrumentalist, built his audience on videos of himself building beats in real time, playing the drums, keys and percussion himself and leaving the seams in.

Exclaim! called him "Toronto's Latest All-Star Beatmaker" back in 2021, and Madlib and Noname are both on record as fans. His own catalogue includes Vignette, a 2022 collaboration with Kaelin Ellis. To see the appeal in one sitting, watch the Tiny Desk (Home) Concert below, where he reworks NPR theme songs alongside Carrtoons, Kaelin Ellis and Kiefer.

Start with: "The Way You Make Me Feel" · Then hear: "Right Here" and "END OF AN ERA"

For the long view

Elaquent

Elaquent has been doing this longer than anyone else here. Sona Elango, based in Guelph, Ontario, has spent well over a decade making warm, patient instrumental hip-hop, across labels like Mello Music Group, URBNET and HW&W. Albums like Forever Is a Pretty Long Time from 2020 and Long Drives to Nowhere are built around a season or a feeling.

Elaquent counts The Kount among his influences.

Start with: "No Hard Feelings" · Then hear: "Three Wishes" and "Moment of Weakness"
