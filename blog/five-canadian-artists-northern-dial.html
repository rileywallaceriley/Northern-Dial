Northern Dial After Dark: Five Canadian Artists to Hear Tonight

September 2, 2026

Northern Dial's library is built for the moment when you want to hear something familiar enough to enjoy and unfamiliar enough to make you stop scrolling. These five Canadian artists offer five very different ways into that feeling.

The selection moves through R&B, hip-hop, pop and independent music without pretending those borders are especially sturdy. At the time of publication, the artists featured here are represented in Northern Dial's library, so you can move from the page to the music without making a second expedition across the internet.

01 · K. Forest

Born in Toronto, K. Forest has spent the past decade building an independent catalogue that moves comfortably between atmospheric R&B, rap and melodic songwriting. His official biography describes a career shaped by both studio work and a serious commitment to his own world-building. Then follow the thread wherever it goes.
Start with: “Forget” in the Northern Dial library.

02 · Enola Bédard

Quebec City-born and now based in Los Angeles, Enola Bédard brings a performer’s full-body perspective to her music. She writes and records, choreographs performances, directs her creative vision and edits her own visual work. That makes her songs feel connected to movement rather than separated from it: pop as something you can hear, see and inhabit.
Start with: “Unavez” in the Northern Dial library.

03 · AHSIA

Philadelphia-born and Vancouver-raised, AHSIA works across soul, R&B, jazz and hip-hop with the kind of range that makes genre labels feel more like suggestions than instructions. Her work also carries a clear social purpose, connecting music with empowerment, community and self-expression. She is exactly the sort of multidimensional Canadian artist a radio library should make room for.
Start with: “patterns” in the Northern Dial library.

04 · Derin Falana

Toronto rapper Derin Falana writes from inside the work: the ambition, uncertainty and persistence involved in trying to build a life around music. His catalogue balances melodic instincts with direct, reflective writing. There is a local connection here, but the appeal is broader—the songs are about momentum, not simply geography.
Start with: “Good Call” in the Northern Dial library.

05 · Haley Smalls

Haley Smalls brings a polished R&B-pop sensibility to the Northern Dial library, with a voice that can carry both intimacy and scale. Her Matches project offers a natural starting point; explore the rest of the record from there. It is contemporary Canadian R&B with enough emotional detail to reward repeat listens.
Start with: “Lost It” in the Northern Dial library.

Hear them on Northern Dial: Listen live from the homepage, or browse the song library to find these artists and whatever has just entered rotation.

Five artists are enough to start a conversation, not enough to finish one. That is the useful thing about a living radio library: there is always another Canadian song waiting just outside the frame.
