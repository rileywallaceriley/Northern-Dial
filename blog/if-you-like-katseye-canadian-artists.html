If You Like KATSEYE, Try These Canadian Artists

November 22, 2026

KATSEYE were assembled in public. Dream Academy, the 2023 competition run by Hybe and Geffen Records, started with more than 120,000 applicants and ended with six members, by way of a training program and a televised final twenty. The Netflix docuseries Pop Star Academy: KATSEYE shows the whole grind. Sophia, Manon, Daniela, Lara, Megan and Yoonchae come from the Philippines, Switzerland, the United States and South Korea. There is no Canadian in the group. That gap is what this list is for.

What KATSEYE sell is performance. "Touch" broke through in 2024 on the strength of a dance people wanted to copy. "Gnarly" arrived in April 2025, split the room clean in half, and then went everywhere anyway. "Gabriela" picked up a Grammy nomination for Best Pop Duo/Group Performance, and the group itself is nominated for Best New Artist. The songs are built to be staged: hooks up front, choreography attached, every member given a distinct job to do.

Canada does not have a KATSEYE. It has the parts, spread across a decade of pop: Kiesza, Tate McRae, Carly Rae Jepsen, LU KALA and Allie X. Five singers who each cover one piece of what KATSEYE do as a group, and who all got there without a televised competition.

Hear the playlist:

For the dance-floor breakout

Kiesza

Kiesza is the precedent. The Calgary singer trained as a dancer first, in ballet and in the Young Canadians troupe, before a knee injury at fifteen closed that road. In 2014, she released "Hideaway" and shot the video in a single take on a Brooklyn street, dancing the full length of it. She filmed it with a broken rib. The song debuted at number one in the UK, and "Giant in My Heart" followed it to number four.

Twelve years on, the "Hideaway" video is still the clearest Canadian example of what KATSEYE do now: a pop song that arrives with its choreography already attached, so the dance becomes the way people learn the song. Sound of a Woman, the album both singles came from, is house music with a singer who can actually keep up with it.

Start with: "Hideaway" · Then hear: "No Enemiesz" and "Giant in My Heart"

For choreography as the whole show

Tate McRae

Tate McRae is the closest structural match on this list. She grew up in Calgary as a competitive dancer, finished third on So You Think You Can Dance at thirteen, and built a singing career without ever putting the dancing down. In our Olivia Rodrigo instalment, she carried the feelings brief.

"Sports car" is the proof. Released in January 2025 ahead of So Close to What, which went on to debut at number one in Canada and the US, it is three minutes of control: whispered verses, a chorus that moves like a routine, and a video choreographed by Sean Bankhead that plays like a fashion shoot with drills in it. "greedy" and "exes," both from Think Later, run on the same engine. McRae performs the way a dancer performs, which is the KATSEYE setting.

Start with: "Sports car" · Then hear: "greedy" and "exes"

For pure pop euphoria

Carly Rae Jepsen

Carly Rae Jepsen comes from Mission, BC, finished third on Canadian Idol in 2007, and then had "Call Me Maybe" happen to her. Most people stopped there. The ones who kept going found Emotion (2015).

"Cut to the Feeling" was written for Emotion and left off it for being too cinematic. Jepsen gave it to the animated film Ballerina, where she also voiced a character, and it became the purest rush in her catalogue: a chorus with no ceiling on it. "Run Away With Me" opens Emotion with a saxophone riff that should be illegal in at least one province. Nobody in this country builds a chorus the way Jepsen does.

Start with: "Cut to the Feeling" · Then hear: "Run Away With Me" and "I Really Like You"

For big-voice confidence

LU KALA

LU KALA was born in Kinshasa, came to Canada at three, and grew up in Toronto's Regent Park neighbourhood and then Ajax. She started as a songwriter for other people, with a credit on Jennifer Hudson's "Dangerous," before deciding the voice on the demos should be the voice on the record.

"Pretty Girl Era" is the calling card: disco-pop confidence with a grin on it. "Lottery," her feature on Latto's 2023 single, put her on the Billboard Hot 100, and "Hotter Now" topped the Canadian iTunes chart and earned a SOCAN No. 1 Song Award for topping CBC Music's Top 20 Countdown. She does it solo, at full volume, with no choreography to hide behind.

Start with: "Pretty Girl Era" · Then hear: "Hotter Now" and "Lottery" (Latto feat. LU KALA)

For theatrical art-pop

Allie X

Allie X is Alexandra Hughes from Oakville, trained in musical theatre at Sheridan College, seasoned in Toronto clubs, and relocated to Los Angeles in 2013 to write songs for a living. The X persona is what happened when a theatre kid got hold of a pop budget: every era a costume change, every show a small piece of staging.

"Super Duper Party People," from Cape God (2020), is exactly what the title promises. "Paper Love" and "Casanova," both from CollXtion II (2017), are the maximalist early work. She has written for Troye Sivan and BTS, among others. KATSEYE's members were cast into their personas on television. Allie X built hers herself, which is why hers keeps changing.

Start with: "Super Duper Party People" · Then hear: "Paper Love" and "Casanova"

Where Should KATSEYE Fans Start?

For the closest transition, start with Tate McRae for the performance match, or Kiesza if you came for the dancing.

Or skip the choosing and hit play: the playlist gathers twenty-one songs, sequenced for listening rather than reading. Start wherever it grabs you and work outward.
