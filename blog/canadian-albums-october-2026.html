Canadian Albums Coming in October 2026: New Releases to Hear This Month

October 1, 2026

Last updated: Oct. 1, 2026

October is stacked with new Canadian music, from veteran songwriters and arena rock to experimental pop, Métis psychedelia and one of Vancouver’s most intriguing new punk bands. Northern Dial is keeping track of the Canadian albums arriving throughout the month, with a few places to start if you’d rather listen than stare at a release calendar.

This page will be updated as new releases are announced or dates change. The goal is simple: give you a useful calendar and a quick way into the records worth hearing.

Bruce Cockburn, The Blood of Others

Out Oct. 16

Bruce Cockburn is doing something unusual for his new album: singing other people’s songs. The Blood of Others is a 15-track collection built from material that has stayed with Cockburn across decades, with the idea for the project stretching back to the 1980s.

Cockburn has said some of the songs reach back to music he encountered before he had even begun writing his own. That makes this less a conventional covers record than a map of the music that helped shape one of Canada’s longest-running songwriters.

Start with: “Fare Thee Well”

Allegories, By Accident, On Purpose

Out Oct. 16

Hamilton duo Allegories return with By Accident, On Purpose, continuing Adam Bentley and Jordan Mitchell’s habit of blurring the boundaries between electronic music, indie rock and stranger atmospheric territory. The record adds another chapter to a project that has always been more interested in texture and mood than easy genre labels.

Early tracks including “They’ll Never Suspect Us” and “Honestly, that’s enough honesty” offer the clearest preview so far. Both lean into the duo’s dense, layered production without losing the melodic pull underneath it.

Start with: “They’ll Never Suspect Us”

Boy Golden, Best of Our Possible Lives (Deluxe)

Out Oct. 16

Boy Golden isn’t replacing February’s Best of Our Possible Lives so much as opening it back up. The Winnipeg songwriter’s deluxe edition adds two new songs and three acoustic versions to the original album.

The first new song, “Pity,” brings some psychedelia into Boy Golden’s rootsy songwriting and circles around the exhaustion that comes from moving too quickly through modern life. If the original album already worked for you, the deluxe release gives it a worthwhile second visit rather than functioning as a simple repackage.

Start with: “Pity”

Art d’Ecco, Scheele’s Green

Out Oct. 23

Art d’Ecco is following 2025’s Serene Demon quickly. Scheele’s Green is the Vancouver artist’s fifth LP, with its current official release date set for Oct. 23.

The seven-minute “The Death of Indie” provides a suitably strange entrance, while “Disappear Here” offers another glimpse of where the record is going. Art d’Ecco has rarely been particularly interested in sitting inside one cleanly defined version of rock music, and the early material suggests that tendency isn’t disappearing here.

Start with: “The Death of Indie”

Sam Drysdale, Purgatory Cove

Out Oct. 23

Sam Drysdale’s debut album arrives after a string of releases that have gradually pulled him back toward guitar-led songwriting. Purgatory Cove uses heartbreak as a kind of in-between place, with friendship, memory and perspective providing the route out.

The record includes “A Place,” “The Window,” “The Astronaut,” “Cold Water” and its title track. Drysdale is also marking the release with a Toronto show on Oct. 22, one day before the album arrives.

Start with: “A Place”

Ghostkeeper, Balloon Moon

Out Oct. 27

Calgary’s Ghostkeeper return with Balloon Moon, the seventh album from the Métis experimental pop group. The 11-song record follows nearly two decades of music that has repeatedly moved between psychedelic rock, folk, pop and more difficult-to-name territory.

Several songs are already available ahead of the album, including “Balloon Moon,” “I Want Love” and “Love and Free Will.” Together, they make the record one of the easier October releases to start exploring before release day arrives.

Start with: “Balloon Moon”

Nickelback, Everything Under the Sun

Out Oct. 30

Yes, Nickelback are back. Everything Under the Sun is the Alberta band’s 11th studio album and their first full-length since Get Rollin’ arrived in 2022.

Among the new material is “Rattle the Cage,” featuring guitarist John 5. Whatever side of the country’s longest-running Nickelback argument you occupy, a new record from one of Canada’s biggest rock exports is going to be difficult to ignore.

Start with: “Rattle the Cage”

PISS, you made me / skin on linen

Out Oct. 30

The record at the other end of Oct. 30 could hardly be more different. Vancouver four-piece PISS release their debut album you made me / skin on linen through Paper Bag Records in Canada and Sub Pop internationally.

PISS combine punk with poetry, sound collage, performance and visual art, using the album to examine violence and the ways traumatic experiences continue shaping a person long after the event itself. Recent single “blocking a scene you can’t remember” gives some indication of how emotionally heavy this record may get, while “time loop at hot slit” shows how much the band can pack into less than two minutes.

Start with: “time loop at hot slit”

What Are We Listening to First?

October’s release schedule works as a pretty good snapshot of why a Canadian music site shouldn’t sound like it covers one Canadian music scene. Within a few weeks, we’ve got Bruce Cockburn revisiting the music that shaped him, Ghostkeeper continuing a distinctly Métis experimental catalogue, Hamilton electronic pop, Winnipeg roots music, Vancouver punk and one of the biggest rock bands Canada has ever produced.

That’s the point of keeping this calendar around. Come back through October as the records arrive, and we’ll keep adding listening picks, Northern Dial artist pages and anything else worth turning up.
