Canadian Women Who Broke Global Pop Open

Six artists, six different routes from Canada to the world, from powerhouse balladry and country crossover to alternative confession, pop-punk and genre-fluid reinvention.

There is no single Canadian route into global pop. This path follows six women whose careers opened distinctly different lanes across the 1990s, 2000s and beyond. It is a story of scale, authorship and reinvention: Québec vocal tradition becoming international pop, country becoming borderless mainstream music, alternative songwriting becoming blockbuster culture, pop-punk becoming a youth language and genre boundaries becoming increasingly porous.

Stop 1 of 6

Céline Dion

Start with scale. Céline Dion moved from a francophone Québec career into one of the largest international pop careers ever built by a Canadian artist, while continuing to carry a distinctly Québec identity into global mainstream culture.

Shania Twain broke a different boundary by making country songwriting and production function as worldwide pop without abandoning the genre's core identity.

Stop 2 of 6

Shania Twain

Shania Twain turned country-pop crossover into a global event. The Woman in Me and Come On Over paired country songwriting with huge hooks and polished production, expanding both the commercial possibilities for Canadian country artists and the international audience for the genre.

View Shania Twain profile →

↓Up next: Sarah McLachlan

Sarah McLachlan changed the surrounding infrastructure by pairing a major singer-songwriter career with Lilith Fair and arena-scale space for women across styles.

Stop 3 of 6

Sarah McLachlan

Sarah McLachlan's catalogue made intimate, adult-alternative songwriting commercially powerful, but her larger significance also includes Lilith Fair. The festival put women-led bills at arena scale and helped challenge an industry culture that routinely treated female artists as programming exceptions.

View Sarah McLachlan profile →

↓Up next: Alanis Morissette

Alanis Morissette turned a more confrontational kind of autobiographical writing into mass-market pop culture.

Stop 4 of 6

Alanis Morissette

Jagged Little Pill made Alanis Morissette a defining voice of 1990s alternative pop by putting anger, vulnerability, irony and self-examination at the centre of a blockbuster record. Its success widened expectations for what a young woman could sound like in mainstream rock and pop.

View Alanis Morissette profile →

↓Up next: Avril Lavigne

Avril Lavigne translated resistance into a younger pop language built from skate-punk attitude, compact hooks and guitar-driven immediacy.

Stop 5 of 6

Avril Lavigne

Avril Lavigne's Let Go made Canadian pop-punk part of the early-2000s global mainstream. Songs such as "Complicated" and "Sk8er Boi" offered a less polished, more guitar-driven alternative to the dominant teen-pop template and helped shape the next generation of pop-rock artists.

↓Up next: Nelly Furtado

Nelly Furtado pushed that openness further by making genre-hopping itself part of a mainstream pop career.

Stop 6 of 6

Nelly Furtado

Nelly Furtado closes the path as a model of genre-fluid Canadian pop. From Whoa, Nelly! and Folklore through Loose, Mi Plan and later independent work, she repeatedly changed musical language while maintaining a recognizable songwriting identity and an international audience.
